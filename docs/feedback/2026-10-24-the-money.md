# How "The money" on /verdict is computed

Report: a $2,000 install cost shows net zero and a payback in months, a $20,000 cost also shows a payback in months, and it was unclear when the HEAT Loan block applies.

## What was wrong

Nothing in the arithmetic produced a wrong number for those two inputs. The display hid why.

- The rebate can never exceed the install cost. At $2,000 the rebate is $2,000, so the net cost is $0 and the payback was printed as "0.0 yr". That is a real result shown in a confusing way.
- At $20,000 the enhanced-tier rebate is still $16,000 (the lower of install cost and the $16,000 cap). Net cost is $4,000 and the payback is 1.5 years, which reads as "months". It is correct for that house.
- The HEAT Loan block always rendered, including a "$0/mo" loan at $2,000 and a loan for a house that saves nothing.
- The loan amount was the net cost with no $25,000 ceiling, so a large quote produced a loan the program does not offer.
- A cost in the link outside $1,000 to $250,000 was dropped silently and the $22,000 average used instead.

## How it works now

Inputs: the install cost (your quote in `ic`, or the Mass Save average of $22,000), the tier and install year, and the yearly saving from your fuel bill versus heat pump electricity.

1. **Rebate.** The Mass Save rebate for your tier and install year, capped at the install cost. For the enhanced tier that cap is Mass Save's rule (lower of install cost and the cap). For the other tiers it is our model assumption, and the card labels it that way.
2. **Net cost.** Install cost minus rebate. Never below $0.
3. **Payback.** Net cost divided by yearly saving, shown in years with one decimal.
   - Saving is zero or negative: "Doesn't pay back".
   - Saving is positive and net cost is $0: "Pays for itself immediately".
   - Otherwise: "x.x years". Beyond 15 years the card says so and the verdict is "not yet".
4. **HEAT Loan block.** Mass Save's 0% HEAT Loan, up to $25,000 (masssave.com, lifetime maximum). masssave.com states no term, so we assume the longest one a participating lender lists, 84 months (7 years). A household's actual term can be shorter. It appears only when the yearly saving is positive and there is something to finance. The amount financed is the net cost, at most $25,000. If the net cost is higher, the block says how much is paid up front. It compares the monthly payment (amount financed ÷ 84) with the monthly saving (yearly saving ÷ 12). It never shows for a house that does not save money, a $0 net cost, or an address with no Mass Save rebate.
5. **Install cost field.** Accepts $1,000 to $250,000. A value in the link outside that band is ignored, and the field says so and names the $22,000 average that was used.

Every figure opens its provenance drawer with the formula, the substituted values and the source. The loan drawer now lists both the net cost and the amount financed.

## Where it lives

- `lib/verdict/money.ts` holds the pure display decisions: payback wording, whether the loan applies, the loan sentence, and the rejected-quote message.
- `lib/calc/index.ts` caps the financed amount at `HEAT_LOAN_MAX_USD` and returns it as `loanAmount`.
- Tests: `lib/verdict/__tests__/money.test.ts`.
